INT. HISTORIAN'S OFFICE – DAY The HISTORIAN works at her desk. A knock. Sailor stands in the doorway. She looks up. HISTORIAN Something wrong with your review? SAILOR No. A beat. SAILOR (CONT'D) Can I ask you something? She gestures to the chair. Sailor sits. SAILOR I was reading the old intervention-reduction trials. The Historian waits. SAILOR (CONT'D) One of them ended on day nineteen. HISTORIAN There were several. SAILOR This one was restored on nineteen. The Archive carries the branch forward after that. HISTORIAN The projected history. SAILOR Right. A beat. SAILOR (CONT'D) Troy called day twenty the day they never saw. The Historian considers that. HISTORIAN He's right. SAILOR But I saw it. HISTORIAN You saw what the Archive projected. SAILOR What's the difference? HISTORIAN One happened. One didn't. Sailor thinks about that. SAILOR Except we make things unhappen all the time. The Historian looks at him. SAILOR (CONT'D) A Drop changes something that already happened. People remember something else. But the record's still there — somebody could always go back and see what happened before. HISTORIAN Yes. SAILOR And some of us don't need to go back and check. HISTORIAN Usually. Sailor studies her. SAILOR So did the first one happen? HISTORIAN Yes. SAILOR Even though nobody remembers it. HISTORIAN You do. A beat. SAILOR So the tree in the woods does make a sound. The Historian considers it. HISTORIAN Apparently. A beat. SAILOR (CONT'D) Just not for everybody. The Historian gives him the faintest smile. SAILOR (CONT'D) That's what I keep getting stuck on. The smile fades — she's listening differently now. SAILOR (CONT'D) It's not that they don't get the truth. It's that they never got a vote on which truth they keep. We pick. They just wake up believing it was always theirs. The Historian says nothing. Something in her stillness has changed register — not defensiveness. Recognition. SAILOR (CONT'D) That's not a design flaw. That's the whole design. HISTORIAN (quiet) I know what the design is. A beat too long. She looks back at her screen — not reading it, just somewhere to put her eyes. Then she recovers, back to procedure. HISTORIAN (CONT'D) Which one was supposed to happen? Wait — she catches Sailor's expression. HISTORIAN (CONT'D) You asked me that. I'm asking you back. SAILOR Which one was supposed to happen? The Historian gives him a strange look. HISTORIAN Supposed to? A beat. HISTORIAN (CONT'D) They happened. All of them. That stops him. SAILOR And this one? HISTORIAN Happening. A beat. Sailor looks down. SAILOR Everybody else gets one. Whatever Continuity leaves them with. That's the life they remember. HISTORIAN Yes. SAILOR And we decide which one that is. The Historian doesn't answer immediately. HISTORIAN Every generation inherits something. Continuity decides which inheritance arrives. Sailor looks at her. SAILOR You're comfortable with that? HISTORIAN Not overly. SAILOR But you work here. HISTORIAN Those aren't opposites. A beat. SAILOR 2187 wasn't a correction. The Historian says nothing. SAILOR (CONT'D) It was a policy. Still nothing. SAILOR (CONT'D) Somebody decided how many people there should be. The Historian looks at him. HISTORIAN Yes. SAILOR And everybody else just got the version that survived the decision. A beat. HISTORIAN Yes. Sailor studies her. SAILOR How many lives have you had? HISTORIAN Five that I remember. I don't know what happened before I came to work here. A beat. HISTORIAN (CONT'D) One of them, I was happy in a way I haven't— She stops herself mid-sentence. Not smoothly. A real stop. HISTORIAN (CONT'D) (recovering, flat) Anyway. Sailor doesn't push. He just watches her a beat longer than he needs to, then lets her have the exit. SAILOR So what does that make us? HISTORIAN The people who remember. SAILOR That's not what I meant. HISTORIAN I know. Silence. The Historian looks at him for a moment. Then reaches for a pad beside her keyboard. She writes something. Tears off only the narrow strip containing what she wrote. Folds it once. Slides it across the desk. Sailor looks at the folded paper. Then at her. Quizzical. The Historian holds his gaze. Nothing in her expression tells him what to do with it. Nothing needs to. Sailor picks up the paper. Without opening it—puts it in his pocket. The Historian turns back to her work. HISTORIAN Anything else I can help you with? Sailor watches her for another beat. SAILOR No. HISTORIAN Good. Sailor stands. Leaves. Only after he's gone—the Historian returns fully to her screen. CUT TO:
